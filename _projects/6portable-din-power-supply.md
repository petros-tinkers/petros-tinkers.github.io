## TL;DR

I had been using a decent bench supply for about a year. It worked well on the desk, but it was so bulky that hanging it on the wall with my tools was impossible. One weekend I designed, printed, and assembled a smaller unit with a magnetic plug. The wall side stays on a DIN rail, and the supply snaps on and off. A second plug lets me feed it from a 12–30 V battery when I want it portable. Input is roughly 5–40 V. Terminals, an XT60 for charging packs, and a fuse box on top so I can replace a fuse without tearing the whole thing apart.

## The old brick

The old supply did its job on the bench. What I couldn't do is hang it on the wall, together with my tools: it was simply too big. I wanted something I could leave mounted, pull off when I needed it elsewhere, and put back without rebuilding anything.

## Magnetic DIN rail mount

The idea is simple: a magnetic connector that delivers power, so you snap the supply onto a wall-mounted DIN rail half and it powers everything. The mount stays on the rail, and the supply comes with you.

I like things modular, so the wall half is not fixed to the pack. Leave the mount on the rail, take the supply, put it back when you're done.

## Second plug for battery power

I printed a second plug as a spare. That one goes on a battery in the 12–30 V range and feeds the same supply. Same magnetic interface, no wall required. So the box is a wall unit when I want it and a portable pack when I don't.

The module itself takes roughly 5 V to 40 V on the input.

## Terminals, XT60, fuse on top

On the box I have a terminal block for day-to-day connections, plus an XT60 so I can charge the packs I'm building. I'm planning a switch there so flipping between charge and run is less of a mess of cables.

For repairability I put a fuse box right on top. I've blown fuses before, and I don't want to tear down the whole enclosure just to replace one.
